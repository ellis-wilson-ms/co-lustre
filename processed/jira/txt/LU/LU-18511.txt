--- Comment (sebastien, Thu, 24 Sep 2026 15:30:53 +0000) ---
What happens is the following.

The client console shows, right after a context flush:

  gss_kt_update()) lustre-MDT0001_UUID at 10.240.43.248@tcp:
  negotiation: rpc err 0, gss err 70000
  ll_close_inode_openhandle()) mdc close failed: rc = -1

gss err 0x70000 is GSS_S_NO_CRED, and rpc err 0 means the

negotiation never reached the server: gss_init_sec_context()

failed on the client because no credential was available. So

gss_kt_update() ends up with -EACCES, flags the context with

PTLRPC_CTX_ERROR_BIT, and sptlrpc_req_refresh_ctx() completes the

request with -EPERM, which dbench reports as a failed operation.

The credential goes missing because of the test itself. 'lfs

flushctx -k' runs kdestroy only after the ioctl that flushes the

contexts, so it destroys the credentials cache while the gss

upcalls triggered by that very flush are still running.

lgss_keyring validates the root TGT in lkrb5_prepare_root_cred(),

but gss_init_sec_context() is called later with

GSS_C_NO_CREDENTIAL, so the credential is only looked up in the

ccache once kdestroy may already have emptied it.

test_90 is meant to check that the client recovers from losing its

gss contexts under load, not from losing its Kerberos credentials,

so we should just drop -k.
