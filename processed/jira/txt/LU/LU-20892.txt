--- Description ---
Problem

nodemap writes exp_banned without exp_lock. exp_banned is a 1-bit field in the same word as exp_hashed and other export flags. The struct comment says that exp_lock protects that word. A concurrent write to another bit in the word can lose the exp_banned update, or the exp_banned write can lose the other update.

	
	

	
	
		Environment

	
	

	master at 550451c0e4 and earlier.

	A server with nodemap active.

	
	
		Reproduction

Source analysis only. We did not reproduce the failure.

1. A client connects. target_handle_connect() calls obd_nid_add(), which sets exp_hashed.

2. At the same time, a nodemap change calls nm_member_reclassify_nodemap(), which sets or clears exp_banned on the same export.

3. Each side reads the word, changes its bit, and writes the word back. The later write removes the earlier update.

	
	

	
	
		Analysis

Writers of exp_banned without exp_lock:

	
	

	nodemap_add_member(), lustre/ptlrpc/nodemap_handler.c

	nm_member_reclassify_nodemap(), the "banning client" and "un-banned client" branches, lustre/ptlrpc/nodemap_member.c

The two results of a lost update:

	exp_hashed goes back to 0 while the export is in obd_nid_hash. class_disconnect() then skips obd_nid_del(), and the hash keeps its export reference. The export leaks.

	exp_banned goes back to its old value. The ban change does not apply.

LU-20403 adds a runtime writer of exp_hashed: ptlrpc_refresh_export_primary() re-keys the export in obd_nid_hash under exp_lock when the client primary NID migrates. Before LU-20403, the main writer ran at connect.

	
	
		Proposed fix

Take exp_lock around each exp_banned write in nodemap_handler.c and nodemap_member.c.
