--- Comment (hornc, Sun, 9 Aug 2026 15:31:25 +0000) ---
Root cause is a test-side race, not an LNet defect.

A router marks an NI down in lnet_update_ni_status_locked() (lnet/lnet/router.c) only once "now >= net->net_last_alive + router_ping_timeout + alive_router_check_interval". net_last_alive has a single runtime update site (lnet/lnet/lib-move.c, in lnet_parse()): an incoming GET on LNET_RESERVED_PORTAL from a non-local source, i.e. an LNet ping or discovery. That same site also sets the NI straight back to LNET_NI_STATUS_UP.

check_router_ni_status() waits exactly alive_router_check_interval + router_ping_timeout, which is the minimum idle period the router needs. There is no slack, so one stray ping arriving during the wait restarts the idle period and the check expires with the NI still up.

The pings are real. In a recent b_es-reviews failure of test_222 (https://testing.whamcloud.com/test_sets/7d760f1b-6556-4876-bc9a-d193ba752e02), the router console shows, at the moment of failure:

  LNet: 1 peer NIs in recovery (showing 1): 10.240.47.154@tcp

10.240.47.154 is not part of that session; its nodes are .158 to .162. The nodes share a flat subnet and load LNet with accept=all, so an unrelated host reached the router, refreshed net_last_alive and re-set the tcp NI to up. Timing agrees: the tcp net was created at 13236.6, so the NI was due down by ~13246.6, but it was still up at 13257.4. The tcp1 net saw no such traffic and went down normally.

This explains why the failure is intermittent and why it moves between subtests. Tests 222 and 223 (do_aarf_enabled_test) and 224 and 225 (the avoid_asym_router_failure=0 variant) all open with check_router_ni_status $router "down" "down", and each has three further calls that can report "tcp/tcp1 should be up|down". The following look like the same defect and can be closed against this ticket:

  LU-19762 sanity-lnet test_223: tcp should be down

  EX-13536 sanity-lnet test_225: tcp should be down

Test-side fix pushed: https://review.whamcloud.com/67867 - wait on a wall-clock deadline of three idle periods instead of one, so a late ping no longer fails the check. Runs that reach the expected state still return immediately.
