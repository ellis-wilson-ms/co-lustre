--- Comment (green, Fri, 24 Jul 2026 18:58:23 +0000) ---
ok, so a lot of this is ai generated, but claude rootcaused this for me with instrumentation and all:

  Root cause, pinned to the end-state (not theory):

  - The failure is a dangling directory entry: source_file5 is returned by

  readdir but lfs path2fid → ENOENT. rm -rf skips the ENOENT child, rmdir →

  ENOTEMPTY, permanently unremovable.

  - The dirent references child FID [0x200000404:0x3b:0x0] — in migrate_dir's

  own sequence — which mdt_cross_open reports "doesn't exist" for the whole run.

  So migration created a child object, inserted a name pointing at it, then the

  object was destroyed, leaving the name dangling.

  - The underflow that flags it is mdo_ref_del at mdd_dir.c:4837 (confirmed

  earlier by stack trace), with the stock nlink==0 (LU-3915) log as

  corroboration.

 

  This race is uninstrumentable on the MDS hot path. Any per-operation CERROR —

  even one filtered to just the three race filenames — slowed the MDS enough to

  drop reproduction to 0/9. That's why I could pin the site but not capture the

  full interleaving, and it's a real constraint on how much further in-kernel

  tracing can go. I fell back to stock + post-mortem, which is non-perturbing

  and gave the end-state facts above.

 

 the ai conclusion:

  The correct fix is a concurrency-atomicity change — making per-entry migration

  atomic with respect to concurrent rename/unlink of that object's names — not

  a lock bit or a point guard. Four targeted point-fixes failing, plus the

  ticket's age, both point the same way. I don't have that design validated, and

  I won't dress up a partial one as the fix.
