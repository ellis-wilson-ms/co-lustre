--- Description ---
Since LU-19388 (bdb39e8390 "osd: remove calls to alloc_file_pseudo()", first in 2.17.0), osd-ldiskfs gives each fake struct file it hands to iterate_dir() or vfs_fsync_range() an LSM blob with security_file_alloc(). The callers of osd_get_filp_for_inode() free it with security_file_free(). The directory iterator does not: osd_it_dir_init() allocates the blob, and osd_it_dir_fini() only calls ->release(). So every directory iteration on an ldiskfs target leaks one lsm_file_cache object. The init error paths have the same gap, and they also leak the separately allocated read buffer, or leave the per-thread one marked in use.

How it shows

lsm_file_cache on the MDS grows without bound while filp stays flat. On a four-MDT VM cluster (Rocky 9, 5.14.0-611.55.1.el9_7, master 31baf2b512) under a rename benchmark, MDS0 reached 1.09M objects in about two hours. Anything that iterates a directory on the MDT counts: client readdir, the emptiness check on rmdir and on a rename over a directory, LFSCK, and OI scrub.

Reproducer

On a fresh mount, from one client:

	make 5000 subdirectories in one directory and remove them;

	list a 2000-entry directory 200 times, clearing the client's MDC lock LRU before each listing.

lsm_file_cache on the MDS grew by 10,259 on master and by 0 with the fix.

At 32 bytes an object the leak is small, about 34 MB per million iterations, but it never stops.

Fix

Free the blob in osd_it_dir_fini(). On the osd_it_dir_init() error paths, free it and give the read buffer back.

This was found while chasing LU-20053, a separate and much larger MDS leak in the kernel's ext4 xattr code.
