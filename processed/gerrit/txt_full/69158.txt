LU-20817 lnet: spread unique portals over match tables

A unique portal picks its match table, and with it the
per-CPT resource lock that guards it, by hashing the peer
NID alone. Every buffer posted for one peer and every
message from that peer then take the same lock, however
many CPTs there are.

That is the bulk path: a client posts each bulk buffer
against the server's NID, so all of its bulk attach, match
and completion work for one server serializes on one lock.
On a 192-core node over 0@lo, where every target shares a
single NID, that lock took about two thirds of the busy CPU
with 128 writers.

A unique portal accepts only exact-match MEs, so attach and
match see the same match bits, and the match bits alone are
enough to spread traffic across every table and every hash
bucket within a table. NID hashing was dropped rather than
kept alongside it, in both lnet_match2mt() and
lnet_mt_match_head(): attach hashes the ME's own NID, but
match hashes lnet_parse()'s primary NID for the same peer,
and the two can differ under multi-rail. That could put the
message in a table or bucket with no matching ME and drop
it before the same-peer fallback in lnet_try_match_md() gets
a chance to run.
