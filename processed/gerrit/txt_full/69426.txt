LU-19823 lod: spread failover groups over the RR array

Without failure domains, the round-robin array only keeps the targets
of each server apart.  The targets of the two servers of an HA pair
can still be next to each other in the array, so consecutive stripes
of a file are on servers that can fail together, or be unavailable
together while one of them fails over to the other.

For the targets without a failure domain, ltd_rr_failover_order()
groups them by the lowest NID of their primary and failover
connections, which is the same for all the targets that can fail over
to each other, and orders the array by group as for failure domains.
lrrt_inferred keeps these groups apart from explicit failure domains
with the same number, also when only some targets have a domain.
All the targets of a server go in the group of its lowest NID, since
the array is ordered only by server if a server is in several groups.
The groups are ranked by NID, first by network and then by address,
and lu_rr_group_order() orders them by the reversed bits of their
rank, so groups with close NIDs, likely in the same network or rack,
are mostly far apart in the array.  Unlike failure domains, this does
not limit where the stripes of a layout or its mirrors may go, but
when other OSTs have failure domains, a slot of a failover group gives
the OSTs of both servers of the group in turn to EC and FLR layouts.
The MDT round-robin array, used for striped directories, is ordered
the same way.  The failover NIDs are read from the import at each
rebuild of the array, so add_conn and del_conn take effect at the next
rebuild.

Add a -F option to lod_rr_test to put the OSSs without a failure
domain in HA pairs, and check that consecutive OSTs in the array are
on different pairs, and a -U option to leave every other OST without a
failure domain.  Without the ordering, "lod_rr_test -f -o 4-64 -s 2-16
-F" finds OSTs of one HA pair next to each other in 1852 of 30512
combinations, and in none with it.
