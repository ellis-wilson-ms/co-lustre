LU-19916 mgs: keep MGS follower config and rules current

An MGS follower serves its clients the copies of the <fsname>-client,
params and sptlrpc logs in the CONFIGS of the target it runs on, but
nothing told it when MGS0 changed them, so its clients kept the old
config until they reconnected.  Nor did it have MGS0's
mgs.srpc.flavor.* rules, which MGS0 keeps in _mgs-sptlrpc: with an
empty rule set tgt_connect_check_sptlrpc() lets an MGS take any
flavor, so a client without a flavor could get the config from a
follower that MGS0 would have refused.

The follower registers as an observer of the MGC so it hears about
config changes; obd_notify() takes a union obd_notify_data to carry
the payload.  It is unregistered before it is freed, since the MGC is
shared by every target on the node and outlives any one of them, and
the MGC holds the observer link for read across every notify:
unregistering takes it for write, so an unmount waits out a notify
already inside the MGS rather than tearing it down underneath it.  The
MGC only notifies for the clds of the target that runs the follower,
whose copies the follower serves.  A follower queues each lock revoke
to its own workqueue, since a revoke waits until every one of its
clients has cancelled the lock, and the requeue thread that notifies
it serves every MGC on the node.

While a follower runs, the MGC also keeps a copy of _mgs-sptlrpc in
its target's CONFIGS, fetched by the requeue thread rather than under
server_mgs_lock(), since MGS0 may be down.  The follower loads the
rules from that copy when it starts and whenever it changes, and
refuses every client until it has one.  It loads them in the notify
itself, which the MGC sends under the cld_lock of that log, so that
the next copy cannot rewrite the log while it is read.  MGS0 now
always keeps an _mgs-sptlrpc, empty if it has no rules, so that a
copy also says there are none.  A follower still sends a client with
an sptlrpc flavor to MGS0.

conf-sanity test_166d checks that a follower whose target has no copy
of _mgs-sptlrpc refuses a client while MGS0 is down, and serves it
once MGS0 is back, test_166e that a client of a follower sees a
set_param -P made on MGS0 without reconnecting, and test_166f that a
follower takes and drops an mgs.srpc.flavor rule set on MGS0.
