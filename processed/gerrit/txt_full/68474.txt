LU-19916 mgs: do not offer a target with no MGS as an MGS

Clients are to learn from the imperative recovery log which server
NIDs they can reach an MGS at (LU-19917).  Not every server runs one:
a target needs "mgs_follower" to start a follower, "nomgs", "nosvc"
and a read-only mount all keep it off, and nothing listens on
MGS_REQUEST_PORTAL there, so an attempt would cost a full connect
timeout rather than being refused.

A target already reports LDD_F_SV_TYPE_MGS to the MGS when mounted
with "mgs", and mgs_nidtbl_write() used to discard it.  Report it when
an MGS runs on the node, keep it, and pass it on in mne_type, so a
client can tell which nodes are worth trying; it now reads mne_type
as a mask rather than comparing it with LDD_F_SV_TYPE_OST.

What a target reports is now whether an MGS that serves its own
filesystem runs on the node, rather than what its mount line asked
for.  mount.lustre adds "mgs" from the on-disk label of a device
formatted with --mgs and "nomgs" does not take it away again, and the
MGC requeue thread can start a follower long after a target mounted -
which now also has the targets notify the MGS of their NIDs again, so
the bit reaches the clients.  Starting a follower at mount does the
same, and so does stopping one, at umount or when an MGT takes over.
tgt_add_notifier() does the same once it has installed the upcall,
when that changed what the target reported, because a follower
started before that has nothing to notify through.  The upcall now
goes on every target's MGC rather than only the first one's, or a
follower fed by a node's second MGC would never notify, and it checks
the workqueue under the notifier lock, because a follower starting at
umount can call it while the last target destroys that workqueue.

One node can hold targets of two filesystems with different MGSes, and
each of those has its own MGC, so "an MGS is running here" would tell
the wrong MGS: a follower replicates only the MGS of the MGC that
feeds it, which is the MGC it observes, and MGS0 serves the targets
whose MGC reaches it at a NID of this node, whether or not the MGT
shares their device.  A target reports the bit when the MGS here is
one of those two.

An older client makes that comparison for equality, and would read a
flagged OST entry as the MDT with the same index, then either drop it
or hand the OST's NIDs to that MDT's import.  So the bit is masked
out for a peer without the new OBD_CONNECT2_MGS_TYPE flag, which every
MGC now sets, a server's as well as a client's.  OBD_CONNECT_FTM
cannot serve: a follower refuses peers without it to keep targets
from registering there, and the server MGC that will feed a follower
its copy of the IR log (LU-19917) needs the bit too.
