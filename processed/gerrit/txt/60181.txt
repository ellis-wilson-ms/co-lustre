LU-9 lod: weighted round-robin QoS allocation

When OST free space is unbalanced, lod_ost_alloc_qos() picks each
stripe at random, weighted by free space less penalties for recently
used OSTs and OSSs.  The random choice only balances allocations on
average, so some OSTs get far more or fewer objects than their share,
and first stripes are not balanced at all.

Use the round-robin array and start index for QoS allocation too, and
map each round-robin index to an OST by weight, so that OSTs are used
in proportion to their free space, and in round-robin order if their
free space is the same.  Each pass through the array spaces its
indices evenly over the total weight, at least as far apart as the
largest weight so that consecutive indices use different OSTs, or one
per OST if the weights are nearly equal.  After every index has been
used as a start, the spacing is shifted by a golden ratio fraction, so
that OSTs whose weight is not a multiple of the spacing get their
share over several passes.  An OST gets at most one stripe of a
layout, so cap the weights at 1/stripes of the total and share the
rest among the other OSTs by weight, and overstriped layouts other
than EC still fall back to round-robin allocation.

Drop the penalties from the OST weights, since they only served to
spread the random choices, so the tgt_penalty values shown by
lod.*.qos_ost_weights no longer affect OST allocation.  qos_prio_free
now moves the weights above the average down toward it, so 100%
allocates in proportion to free space, and 0% uses the OSTs with more
than the average free space equally.  Fuller OSTs keep a weight in
proportion to their free space.  lod.*.qos_ost_weights shows the
weights that are used.  Scale the weights down by a power of two if
needed so their sums cannot overflow.  MDT QoS allocation and the
qos_threshold_rr and qos_prio_free defaults are unchanged.

An OST that can't be used for a stripe now only moves that stripe on
to the next OST in the array.  Before, the random choice skipped the
weight of avoided, used or failing OSTs differently than it summed
it, so allocation often failed and fell back to round-robin when
OSTs were offline (LU-20526).  Also return success when a layout
using the default striping gets fewer stripes than requested but at
least the minimum, instead of -ENOSPC, which skipped the round-robin
fallback and failed the create.

Add lod.*.qos_wrr_enable, 1 by default, which can be set to 0 to pick
the OSTs at random by the same weights, without the penalties, as a
fallback in case of problems with weighted round-robin allocation.

Add man pages for lod.qos_threshold_rr, lod.qos_prio_free,
lod.qos_maxage and lod.qos_ost_weights, and their mdt_ versions, and
for lod.qos_wrr_enable.  Also fix lod.*.qos_maxage, which showed
mdt_qos_maxage.

Add a QoS mode to lod_rr_test and run it in sanity test_51g, and add
sanity test_51h to run test_51d with qos_threshold_rr=0, and to check
that qos_wrr_enable=0 uses all OSTs.  Repeat the creates of test_51d
if an OST starts a new FID sequence during them, since it has no
precreated objects for a while.  lod_rr_test with 3-48 OSTs on 2-4
OSSs, and more free space on the last OSS, finds these fail to put
4/5-5/4 of each OST's capped share of objects on it, or 2/3-3/2 of its
share of first objects.  Before is modelled as the weighted random
choice without penalties:

                           before         after
  2x free space            335/3516       0/3516
  4x free space            1665/3516      0/3516
  10x, qos_prio_free=90%   2305/3516      0/3516
  2x, qos_prio_free=50%    160/3516       0/3516
  10x on one OST, 1-4 OSS  2712/4689      0/4689

For example 16 OSTs on 2 OSSs, 4 stripes, 4x free space on OSS1:

             OSS0 OSTs            OSS1 OSTs
             objects  #0 objects  objects  #0 objects
  before     167-191  34-44       605-636  136-188
  after      156-164  39-41       640      160
  wanted     160      40          640      160
