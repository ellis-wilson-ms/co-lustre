LU-20590 ldlm: pin a lock out of the client LRU

An in-flight IO can depend on a client lock without a mode reference
on it. The LRU age reaper, the cache policy and the pool shrinker can
then drop that lock.

Add ldlm_lock_pin_lru() and ldlm_lock_unpin_lru(). A caller can now
exclude a granted client lock from voluntary reclaim. Pins nest
through the new l_lru_pins count, and the exemption ends on the last
unpin. The kernel-doc holds the full contract.

The only consumer is the child patch. That patch pins the MDC ibits
layout lock of a file whose IO restarts with no progress. The
interface never pins an extent lock, and the osc layer does not use
the client LRU helpers at all.

The pin sets LDLM_FL_NO_LRU and removes the lock from the LRU in one
critical section under the resource lock. An atomic count cannot take
the place of that lock. The pin is one test-and-act over three items:
l_lru_pins, LDLM_FL_NO_LRU and the LRU list membership. The scan in
ldlm_prepare_lru_list() runs the mirror of that section under the same
resource lock. The scan commits when it sets LDLM_FL_CBPENDING and
LDLM_FL_CANCELING, and it cannot go back after that point. A later
read of a counter therefore cannot repair a lost race. The cost is
small, because only a starving IO takes a pin. One syscall takes at
most four pins.

The pin adds no mode reference, and it changes no policy data. A
blocking callback, an eviction and a namespace cleanup all still
cancel the lock at once. No remote peer waits on a pin. An extent, a
client page and the bulk IO accounting see no change. A caller can
therefore hold a pin for the length of a syscall.

An unpin which files the lock again also runs the pool recalculation.
The unpin can therefore sleep, and it can select unrelated locks of
the namespace for cancellation. A final decref does the same.

l_lru_pins takes the spare u16 beside the other LRU policy fields, so
struct ldlm_lock does not grow. The lock dump prints the count, so a
reader can tell a pin from an enqueue exemption.
