LU-16330 lod: use allow_signal() for killable sem

When lod_ost_alloc_qos() cannot get lq_rw_sem it arms a 2s timer
whose callback sends SIGKILL to bound the wait in
down_write_killable() and fall back to round-robin allocation.
Before sleeping it installed a SIG_DFL disposition for SIGKILL with
kernel_sigaction().

With a SIG_DFL handler sig_fatal() is true, so the first time the
timer ever fired in the life of a given MDT service thread,
complete_signal() started a group exit and latched
SIGNAL_GROUP_EXIT in signal->flags.  That first wakeup still
worked, but nothing ever clears SIGNAL_GROUP_EXIT on a kthread
that does not exit (flush_signals() only empties the signal
queues).  Since kernel v6.1 commit 9a95f78eab70 ("signal: Drop
signals received after a fatal signal has been processed"), also
backported to RHEL 9, prepare_signal() drops every signal sent to
a task with SIGNAL_GROUP_EXIT set.  All later timer wakeups in
that service thread were therefore silently discarded:
down_write_killable() slept through the whole 20s OST failover
pause instead of falling back to round-robin after 2s, and
recovery-small test_152 failed intermittently.

Use allow_signal(SIGKILL) instead.  It installs the SIG_KTHREAD
handler, so the timer's signal is still delivered and wakes the
killable sleeper, but sig_fatal() is false and SIGNAL_GROUP_EXIT
is never set, keeping the wakeup working for the whole life of the
service thread.  disallow_signal() restores SIG_IGN and also
flushes a SIGKILL that arrived just as the semaphore was granted.

Note kthreads do not start with signals blocked: kthreadd() sets
all handlers to SIG_IGN via ignore_signals() and current->blocked
is empty; neither kernel_sigaction() nor allow_signal() touches
the blocked mask.
