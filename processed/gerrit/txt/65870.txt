LU-20258 ec: reject EC layouts with k+m > 256

Lustre EC uses a Cauchy matrix construction over GF(2^8) (see
gf_gen_cauchy1_matrix() in lustre/ec/ec_base.c).  Cauchy matrix
theory requires k+m distinct field elements, and GF(2^8) has
exactly 256, so the construction is mathematically bounded by
k + m <= 256.

Until now the user-space validation layers enforced the per-axis
caps independently but not their sum.  A user could ask for e.g.

    lfs setstripe --ec-expert --ec 255+15 ...

(k+m = 270 > 256) and have the layout accepted, only to hit a
runtime EC failure or get a non-MDS code from the encoder.

Tighten validation to reject k + m > 256 at every user-space
entry point:

 - lustre_user.h: add LOV_EC_MAX_TOTAL_STRIPES = 256 next to
   the existing LOV_EC_MAX_DATA_STRIPES / _CODING_STRIPES macros,
   with a comment naming the Cauchy/GF(2^8) origin.
 - lfs.c parse_ec_stripe_count(): reject data + parity > 256 with
   a descriptive error.  Placed before the --ec-expert recommended-
   limits check so it applies even with --ec-expert (the bound is
   theoretical, not a policy choice).
 - liblustreapi_layout.c llapi_layout_comp_add_ec(): same check
   for callers that bypass lfs.
 - lfs-setstripe.1: document the k+m <= 256 constraint in both
   the general --ec section and the --ec-expert section.
 - llapi_layout_test.c test47: update Test 3 to expect failure at
   dstripe+cstripe = 255+15 = 270 (was expected to succeed); add
   Test 4 covering the boundary case dstripe+cstripe = 241+15 = 256.

MDS-side enforcement is added by a companion LU-20258 patch in
lustre/lod/lod_lov.c (lod_verify_striping); the two patches
together provide defense in depth so layouts that bypass userspace
(hand-crafted xattrs, replay paths) are also rejected.

Background: this was surfaced during review of LU-20238 (GFNI EC
acceleration) PS6 on Gerrit 65798, where Andreas Dilger asked
whether k+m <= 256 was a GFNI, ISA-L, or generic Lustre limit.
The answer is that it is a theoretical Cauchy-matrix-over-GF(2^8)
bound; lifting it would require a larger field and is out of
scope here.
